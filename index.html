01 / THE SHORT VERSION

Same genus.
Different branches.

They’re all in the genus Allium, along with garlic, shallots, chives, and ramps. What changes is the species, the way they grow, and which bit we like to eat.

The biggest naming trap: “green onion” describes a market form, not one perfectly tidy species.

BRANCH AAllium fistulosum

The bunchers

Scallion / green onion, Welsh onion, Japanese bunching onion, and daepa. They stay slender or thick-shanked rather than making a big round bulb.

→
BRANCH BAllium cepa

The bulb makers

Regular onions and shallots. A young bulb onion may also be sold as a “green onion” or “spring onion,” which is where the labels get slippery.

+
BRANCH CAllium ampeloprasum

The leek branch

Garden leeks have broad, flat leaves arranged like a fan and a thick layered shank. They’re a distinct crop—not just an overgrown scallion.

HOLLOW LEAVES ↑

Scallion / green onion

THE SAME THING—USUALLY

In North American cooking, these names are normally interchangeable. The plant is often A. fistulosum, but it can also be an immature A. cepa.

LOOK
Pencil-thin, hollow green tubes, tiny white base
TASTE
Fresh, bright, lightly oniony
BEST AT
Garnish, salads, quick cooking
02
THICC SCALLION ENERGY

Daepa / giant green onion

SCALLION’S BIG SIBLING

Korean daepa (대파) is a large type of bunching onion, generally A. fistulosum. It has hollow leaves and an extra-long, sturdy white shank.

LOOK
Much thicker than a scallion; still tubular
TASTE
Oniony, sweetens beautifully with heat
BEST AT
Soups, stocks, grilling, pa-kimchi
03
FLAT LEAF FAN ↑

Leek

A DIFFERENT BRANCH

A leek isn’t a giant green onion. It has broad, flat, overlapping leaves, a layered shank, and usually belongs to A. ampeloprasum.

LOOK
Wide, flat leaves in a V-shaped fan
TASTE
Mild, sweet, earthy; soft when cooked
BEST AT
Braising, soups, pies, slow cooking
04
ACTUAL BULB ↓

Regular onion

THE BULB MAKER

The familiar yellow, white, or red bulb onion is A. cepa. It stores energy in a round, layered bulb and is usually harvested mature.

LOOK
Round papery bulb; greens often trimmed
TASTE
Sharp raw; deep and sweet cooked
BEST AT
Nearly everything, honestly

05 / THE EXTENDED FAMILY

Other relatives
worth inviting

All are in the genus Allium. They each found their own niche.

BULB CLUSTER

Shallot

Allium cepa, Aggregatum Group

Onion’s close cousin—or, taxonomically, part of onion’s own species. Smaller, clustered bulbs with a delicate flavor.

HERB MODE

Chives

Allium schoenoprasum

Fine hollow leaves used like an herb. Snip them; don’t ask them to carry a stew.

CLOVE MACHINE

Garlic

Allium sativum

A bulb made of cloves, with a stronger sulfurous punch. Its curly flower stalk is the garlic scape.

WILD ONE

Ramp

Allium tricoccum

A North American woodland allium with broad leaves and a bold garlic-onion flavor. Harvest responsibly.

Three bonus naming traps

Spring onion

Often means a young bulb onion with a small round bulb. In some regions, it’s just another name for scallion.

Garlic chives

Allium tuberosum: flat, grassy leaves and a garlicky taste. Not regular chives, not baby garlic.

Pearl + cipollini onions

Small shapes and cultivars of bulb onion—not separate branches of the family tree.
